Scholarship Essay Help

Free tips for writing scholarship and financial aid essays. Education only.

Common scholarship essay prompts

  • "Tell us about yourself."
  • "Describe a challenge you overcame and what you learned."
  • "Why do you deserve this scholarship?"
  • "How will this scholarship help you reach your goals?"
  • "Describe your involvement in your community or school."

A simple structure that works

  1. Hook (1–2 sentences) — Start with a specific moment, not a broad statement.
  2. Context — Who you are and what matters to you.
  3. The challenge or story — What happened, what you did, what you learned.
  4. Connection — How this connects to your education and career goals.
  5. Closing — Why this scholarship matters and a brief thank-you.

Quick do's and don'ts

Do Don't
Answer the specific prompt asked Reuse an essay without editing it for each scholarship
Be specific with examples and details Write what you think judges want to hear
Proofread aloud and ask someone to review Submit the first draft
Stay within the word count Exaggerate or make things up
Show growth and what you learned Focus only on hardship without a forward-looking plan

Before you submit

  • Read the prompt one more time and make sure you answered it.
  • Check spelling, grammar, and word count.
  • Confirm your name and contact information are on the essay if required.
  • Save a copy of every essay you submit — you can adapt it later.
