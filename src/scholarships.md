# Scholarships & Grants Directory

A directory of free scholarship and financial aid resources. Every link goes to the official program or government source. Education only — amounts and deadlines change, so confirm on the official page before applying.

## Federal grants and aid (start here)

| Program | What it is | Award | Official link |
|---|---|---|---|
| FAFSA | Free Application for Federal Student Aid — unlocks grants, work-study, and federal loans | Varies (need-based) | [fafsa.gov](https://fafsa.gov) |
| Federal Pell Grant | Need-based grant for undergraduate students | Up to $7,395 (2024–25) | [studentaid.gov/pell](https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/grants/pell) |
| FSEOG | Campus-based grant for students with exceptional need | $100–$4,000 per year | [studentaid.gov/fseog](https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/grants/fseog) |
| TEACH Grant | Grant for students who agree to teach in a high-need field | Up to $4,000 per year | [studentaid.gov/teach](https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/grants/teach) |
| Iraq and Afghanistan Service Grant | For students whose parent/guardian died in service in Iraq or Afghanistan | Up to Pell Grant max | [studentaid.gov/iasg](https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/grants/iraq-afghanistan-service) |

## State aid

Each state has its own grant and scholarship programs. Start at your state's higher education agency — find contacts through the U.S. Department of Education: [ed.gov state contacts](https://www.ed.gov/about/contacts/state.html).

## Tips

- File the FAFSA every year — it is free at fafsa.gov. Never pay to file it.
- Apply to many smaller local scholarships; they often have fewer applicants.
- Keep copies of everything you submit.
- Ask your high school counselor or college financial aid office about local awards.
