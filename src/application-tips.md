# Scholarship & Financial Aid Application Tips

A step-by-step beginner's guide to applying for scholarships and financial aid. Education only.

## 1. Start with the FAFSA

The [FAFSA](https://fafsa.gov) is free and opens the door to federal grants, work-study, and loans. Many states and colleges also use it for their own aid.

- File at [fafsa.gov](https://fafsa.gov) — never pay a site to file it for you.
- Create an [FSA ID](https://studentaid.gov/fsa-id) first (you and a parent, if you're a dependent student).
- File every year you're in school, as early as possible after it opens (typically October 1).
- Check your state and college deadlines — they are often earlier than the federal deadline.

## 2. Get organized before you apply

- Make a spreadsheet or use the [deadline tracker](deadline-tracker.html): program, deadline, requirements, link, status.
- Gather documents early: tax returns, W-2s, Social Security numbers, school transcripts, and a list of extracurricular activities.
- Ask for letters of recommendation at least 3–4 weeks before the deadline. Give your recommender your résumé and the scholarship details.

## 3. Search smart

- Start with your school counselor and college financial aid office — they know local awards.
- Use official sources: [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov), [bigfuture.collegeboard.org](https://bigfuture.collegeboard.org), your state higher education agency.
- Beware of scholarships that ask for an application fee or "guarantee" an award — those are scams. Report them to the FTC at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

## 4. Apply widely and early

- Apply to national scholarships and smaller local ones (local awards often have fewer applicants).
- Reuse essays where the prompts are similar, but always tailor names and details to each program.
- Submit before the deadline — technical problems happen, and late applications are usually rejected.

## 5. After you apply

- Track each application in your spreadsheet (submitted, decision, award amount).
- If you win, send a thank-you note to the sponsor.
- If your financial situation changes (job loss, medical bills), contact your college financial aid office — they can sometimes adjust your aid.
